GCP rents a genuine single A100 80GB or H100 80GB, the way to train on one big NVIDIA card instead of a whole 8-GPU AWS node. Veri runs GCP training on Flex-start VMs (Google's Dynamic Workload Scheduler): the job waits in a queue for GPU capacity, and once it has its VM, GCP does not preempt it.

## GPU types

| GPU type | VRAM | Architecture | GPUs available | Regions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `A100-80GB` | 80 GB | NVIDIA Ampere | 1 | us-central1, us-east4, us-east5 |
| `H100-80GB` | 80 GB | NVIDIA Hopper | 1 | us-central1, us-east4, us-west4 |

Any other `gpu_count` is rejected at submit with a `400`. Veri picks the zone: a `region` on a GCP job is rejected at submit (choosing a region is AWS-only).

## Pricing

$3.00 per GPU-hour for A100 80GB and $5.99 per GPU-hour for H100 80GB (October 2026). Bare `veri gpu list` shows the current rates (GCP has no live availability view).

Billing starts when the VM has its GPU. The wait for capacity is not billed, and a job that never gets a GPU (`CAPACITY_UNAVAILABLE`) is not billed at all.

## Submitting a job

```python
job = client.training_jobs.create(
    base_model="Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct",
    dataset_id=dataset.id,
    method="sft_text",
    output_name="qwen-gcp-sft",
    gpu_type="A100-80GB",
    gpu_count=1,
    provider="gcp",
)
```

From the CLI, `veri train my_job.py --provider gcp`, or `provider.name = "gcp"` in a [config file](/cli/training).

## Waiting for capacity

A GCP job stays `queued` while Veri looks for capacity. Veri asks the zones one at a time and keeps each request open for its share of about 15 minutes in total (at least 90 seconds per zone). The first zone with a free GPU wins and the job moves to `provisioning`.

If no zone has a GPU in that time, the job fails with `CAPACITY_UNAVAILABLE` and is not billed. Resubmit later, try the other GPU type, or use another provider (a single H100 is also on [Vast.ai](/training/providers/vast) and [DigitalOcean](/training/providers/digitalocean)).

## One GCP GPU at a time

Veri runs one GCP GPU at a time for now. A GCP job submitted while that GPU is in use waits in `queued`, records one `waiting_for_provider_gpu` event, and launches when the GPU is released, a few minutes after the job using it ends. Nothing is billed while it waits. To start sooner, cancel it and submit on another provider.

## When GCP ends the VM

Flex-start VMs are not preempted, but GCP can still end one before the job finishes: after a host error, for host maintenance, or at the 24-hour maximum run duration that Veri sets on every GCP VM (the same 24-hour limit every job has on its running time). The job then fails with one of these codes:

| `error.code` | What happened | Billing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `provider_reclaimed` | GCP deleted the VM, or stopped it after a host error, for host maintenance or for a preemption, before the job finished | A platform failure (`error.attribution` is `platform`): the job's cost is credited back automatically. `custom_script` jobs are reviewed by hand |
| `max_run_duration_reached` | GCP ended the VM at its 24-hour maximum run duration | Charged, like any job that reaches the running-time limit (`error.attribution` is `customer`). Lower `max_steps` or split the run |

Either way the job keeps the checkpoints it saved, and `error.message` says how to continue. Resume from the newest checkpoint as a new job:

```bash
veri jobs resume job_abc123                 # newest ready checkpoint
veri jobs resume job_abc123 --provider vast # continue on another provider
```

or pass `resume_from="job_abc123@<step>"` on create (`GET /v1/training_jobs/{id}/checkpoints` lists the steps). `resume_from` applies to managed jobs only. See [Checkpoints and resume](/training/checkpoints).
